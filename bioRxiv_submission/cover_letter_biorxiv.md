## Significance Statement

This study presents the first comprehensive single-cell transcriptomic analysis of sleep deprivation across three mouse brain regions, identifying Pomc as a central regulatory hub whose expression collapses by ~80% during acute sleep loss. We construct a Pomc-centered gene regulatory network, perform in silico knockout via network propagation, discover a Pomc-Pcsk2 feed-forward regulatory loop through multi-method validation, and identify a six-gene biomarker panel (AUC = 0.931) for sleep deprivation state classification. All findings are validated in two independent external datasets (Spearman r = 0.728, p < 0.001).

## Key Findings

1. **Pomc** is the most significantly altered gene in the hypothalamus during sleep deprivation (log2FC = −4.08, padj = 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁵⁷), with expression collapsing ~80% and fully recovering upon sleep
2. **Pomc-Pcsk2 feed-forward regulatory loop** discovered and validated by three independent computational methods (Spearman GRN, TF activity inference, gradient boosting regression)
3. **Six-gene biomarker panel** (Dbp, Nr3c1, Pomc, Rbm3, Rnaset2a, Tsc22d3) achieves AUC = 0.931 for classifying sleep-deprived vs control states
4. **Cross-dataset, cross-technology validation** in two independent RNA-seq datasets (GSE211088, GSE237419) confirms consistent directional changes (Spearman r = 0.728)
